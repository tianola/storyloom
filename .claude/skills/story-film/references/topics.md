# Kinds of film and how each one is built

Pick the kind first; it decides who we follow, how the film opens and how it ends. Many topics fit two kinds: pick
the one whose protagonist has the strongest documented problem.

| Kind | Example in the references | Who we follow | Opening shape (script.md §2) | Ending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Discovery against resistance | Mullis (PCR); our Semmelweis | the discoverer, and the people who doubt him | person with a problem, or stakes first | closure: vindication and its price |
| 2 Engineering crisis with a clock | Citicorp | the engineer who finds the flaw | person with a problem in 12 s | closure, then a re-hook ("who was the student?") |
| 3 Invention that turned harmful | Midgley / Patterson | the inventor, and a second person who later proves the harm | thesis without a name | callback irony ("including himself") |
| 4 Investigation of an event | Flight 103 | the investigators, through clues and witnesses | stakes before names | open case: name what is unknown |
| 5 How an institution or system works | Secret Service | a frame story ("a shooter is taking aim") revisited every 4-6 min | stakes before names | the frame closed |
| 6 How a product or machine works | Coca-Cola Freestyle; fiber optics; jet engines | "you" in a place, or the thing on its journey, or a relay of inventors each fixing the last one's problem | "you" in a place, or demonstration | the money / numbers question from the top answered |
| 7 An abstract idea | Dirac | the person whose result shocked a room, and the room | a scene with the name withheld | callback to the room |

## Building notes per kind

1. **Discovery against resistance.** The belief of the time is the guess; the evidence the hero gathers is the
   reversal; the institution's refusal is the cost. Keep the hero human (wrong about something, difficult, tired).
   Give the doubters their reasons in the record's words; nobody is a cartoon villain. Places: where the work happens
   (ward, lab), where the hero lives, where the doubters meet.
2. **Engineering crisis.** A date, a threshold and the people at risk in the first 15 s. Each technical idea is the
   answer to "why could it fail?". Show the decision and its cost (money, reputation, safety) and keep the clock
   visible (hurricane season, a launch date).
3. **Invention turned harmful.** Two threads: the inventor's success story and the person who measures the harm.
   Hand off between them at every chapter boundary. The science of the harm arrives through the second person's
   measurements.
4. **Investigation.** Present tense reconstruction of the event, then clue by clue; each clue has a physical object
   with a number or name on it. Witness voices where documented. End on what is still unknown, not on a guess.
5. **Institution or system.** A hypothetical frame story cuts in every 4-6 minutes with a one-line handle; the
   mechanism sections explain what happens in that frame at each moment; close the frame explicitly.
6. **Product or machine.** If there is no single hero, follow "you" through a place you return to, or follow the thing
   on its journey (a photon down a fibre, a token through a chip), or tell a relay of people, each solving the
   problem the last one left. Show the real object before the animation; demonstrations beat analogies.
7. **Abstract idea.** Stakes come from other people's reactions, not from the narrator asserting importance. Never more
   than 5 minutes of theory without a person beat; give the theory a bigger stage (a room, a model, a demonstration).

## Living-world requirements for any kind

- The film has **at least three places** that return, each with a world-bible entry (staging.md §5) and a time of day.
- **At least two named people** (machine-checked), plus representative people (a mother, a student, a shopkeeper)
  who carry emotion without inventing history.
- **The narrator's home spot** (a rooftop, a desk, a corner of the lab) where he speaks to us at section starts.
- **A running object or place** that returns with a new meaning (a lantern, two doors, a ledger).
